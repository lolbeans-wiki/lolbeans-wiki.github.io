1.1 - Introduction, History, and Overview

Lolbeans.io is a game released on October 14th, 2020 with the main Battle Royale gamemode. Tag came out four years later, releasing on April 10th 2025. Serial, the game’s developer, opened a play test the month before on March 7th, previewing the new gamemode. Historically, Tag started before with the Lolbeans community creating tag games using the Community Levels gamemode. People would make custom stages for tagging, and battle it out with friends. Later, it would become an official gamemode.

We all know the classic playground game tag; it is quite a basic premise, so how could this be fun when translated into the medium of video games? It is about the implementation, which is unique across all the other tag games. Its defining feature is a proximal-point system, in which you gain points the closer you are to the taggers (known as ‘Hunter’ in this game,) fighting for the most points at the end of each round.

Tag was popular at first given the excitement around a new gamemode, as well as it being school season at that time (as a lot of people play at school,) but it has seen a decline ever since due to a lack of updates and an overall fall in the general Lolbeans player base. After release, it got a minor update changing the existing maps in various ways, (I will go into this more in section two.) Then it got another map: Construction, on June 6th of the same year. It had seemed that this gamemode would be regularly updated, with more exciting maps and features as time went on; over one year later, it has yet to get another update, much to the disappointment of me and the rest of the Tag community.

One thing I will admit is that with only four maps and no updates in over a year, the gamemode has risks of getting stale, especially if you do not hold the same amount of love for it that I have. However, with discoveries still being made in terms of tricks and glitches, and the satisfaction of juking people, it can still feel fresh and amazing. Tag is easy to get into for its intuitive simplicity, but has a high skill ceiling as you need to sharpen your juking skills over time, and learn all of the tricks. With this, it has offerings for both veterans and newcomers.

1.2 - Basic Information

Now that you know the history and current situation of the gamemode, I will talk about the gamemode itself. There are upwards of eight players, with two of those eight being Hunters. Rounds begin with a three second countdown, then last for three minutes. After they end, there is a fifteen second intermission time in which it displays the final player rankings for the round. Tag goes on for as long as you stay in the server, unlike Battle Royale which plays three rounds before the game ends. This is because winning in Tag does not net coin or exp rewards like Battle Royale.

Here are the UI elements. The chat box in the top left is self-explanatory. The meter at the bottom displays the amount of points being gained (note that this disappears while you are Hunter, because you gain no points while being in this state.) On the top right is a leaderboard displaying every player and their point totals, along with their player status which is shown by the colour of the player’s name and rank—white for Runner, and red for Hunter.

The objective is to get the most points. For the first 5 seconds of the round, you gain no points, then you gain points every second, getting more points the closer you are to the Hunters at an upward of ten points per second (where you basically have to touch them.) Points can be gained in decimals as well, which you can see by hovering your mouse over the point total during the intermission.

The point mechanic is a big part of what makes this gamemode so fun. It forces players to learn juking, rather than simply hiding or trying to survive the longest. It requires both good mechanics and predictions to win.

As alluded to, there are two types of players: Runners and Hunters. Runners are the default player, and Hunters are the taggers. You can tell somebody is a Hunter by the red name and rank on the leaderboard, or by the red bubble and name tag by their character. While being Hunter, you stop gaining points. Hunters have 20% more than the default speed. Touching a Runner as a Hunter passes the Hunter status to them, stunning the person for around 2.4 seconds in what is known as a ragdoll.

There are four maps as of now: Construction, Color City, Supermarket, and Teleport City (The last three are the file names. For Construction, the file name is Construction Site, but I say Construction as that is the official name given in the update log on the website.) Each map has different obstacles and tricks which makes map knowledge incredibly important. Learn the intricacies of each map (which can just be achieved from attentive and constant play), and use them to your advantage against less knowledgeable players to come out on top. The map shifts in sets of two, meaning you play two rounds on each in the order I listed them.

1.3 - Basic Mechanics

The mechanics are deceptively simple in this game. The only movement options you have are jumping and moving. This is limiting in a way which inspires resourcefulness. It makes it so it would be even more advantageous to learn the maps in and out. The fun is utilizing tricks and glitches which stretch how far one can go with such little mechanics. You can even challenge yourself by prohibiting jumping. The jump height is deliberately less than the player height, making it so you cannot simply jump over Hunters.

There is also the ragdoll state: it is a period of inactionability (You cannot move or tag people) that lasts for around 2.4 seconds. It is called ragdoll because your physics get weird, you get down on the ground, and it leads to certain glitches. Ragdoll is primarily activated when getting tagged. You also get it from hitting special obstacles such as puddles or stop signs, getting hit by something like a wrecking ball or hammer, falling from a great enough height, or in niche cases colliding with another player or wall. It is important to note that the ragdoll state can be activated during a ragdoll, which allows you to stun players infinitely in certain cases.

Uniquely in tag, you can zoom in and out using the scroll function on a keyboard. This allows you to gain more visual information on where players and Hunters are.

1.4 - How to Win

The best way to win is to generally be gaining at least six points per second if you are a beginner. Even seven points allows you to keep a nice amount of distance. I find that there is a lot of lee way come out on top because the average player does not optimize for points. You do not need to be very mechanical or skilled to win, you just need to be calculating and smart about how you gain your points.

When tagged, go after someone who is of a lower skill level, or is pretty close to you. Try to spend as little time tagging someone as possible. One mistake that can cause people to trip up including myself is to try to tag the person in first place. This person is usually decent, and may take a while to tag, leading you to wasting a lot of time and widening the gap between your point totals even more. So overall just go for the easy person and try to catch up.

If the competition is really intense, you will need to be gaining 8-9 points to catch up. Find AFK players, farm bots if possible, use tricks mentioned in part two, or just be good enough to avoid players even from that close of a distance. As a goal to set for yourself, try to reach at least 1000 points per game on average.

1.5 - Juking

Juking skill is determined by how long you can avoid being tagged while also still gaining a good amount of points. It adds a risk reward system to the game. You can either keep your distance, losing out on a lot of points but barely getting tagged, or stay close, gaining many points but being at much more risk. This tight rope players have to walk is another component of the game that keeps it interesting. Here I will explain beginner methods that will help you get tagged less while also gaining adequate points.

The easiest principle to follow is that just like in many other games, high ground is a treasure in Tag. With short jump heights and stubby proportions of the characters, it is easy to get height over Hunters, allowing you to be close but also safe. For example, on Teleport City there is this ramp section where Hunters will have to go all the way around to get you. This is also the basis of many of the easiest tricks. The main ones being the barrel and bin jukes, where you gain height by jumping off a barrel or bin, allowing you to go over Hunters.

A more bare bones way of juking is simply tricking the Hunter into thinking you are going one way, then going the other, or just doing something unexpected like spinning around. This adds an element of meso to the game; adapt to habits and predict accordingly. This skill is important especially if the person you are being chased by has similar to or higher trick-knowledge than you. Ultimately there is not an exact science to this, juking depends of many factors such as ping, reaction time, and luck, as nobody is a fortune teller; it requires a lot of practice to get better at.

1.6 - Deeper Dive into Mechanics

There are more advanced mechanics in this game that makes the game a lot more engaging once you get a grasp on them. These are less obvious, but more powerful things to know that will make the game easier.

First is speed. Normally you cannot increase your speed, always moving at the default, but there is a way of building speed and maintaining it. On sloped surfaces, with enough height, if you jump while going down them, you gain some extra speed (at times this also stops ragdoll when falling from a great height.) You can also gain speed from things that launch you such as the hammers on Teleport City, wrecking ball on Construction, and the bouncepads on Supermarket. Now usually you lose this speed after landing, but if you keep jumping, you maintain a portion of that speed, allowing you to zoom through maps, this is called the B-Hop.

Another aspect is the jump physics, they are strange, and can be abused in insane ways and allows for otherwise unfathomable glitches. Normally a jump is pretty short, not going over players, but there are ways to make it higher. Consecutive jumps in some window of time will lead to greater height. For example, jumping from the ground and then off another object quickly enough will send you pretty high. Chaining jumps together also gives a bit more height, this most crucially allows you to more easily jump off the railings on Construction. Jumping from a sloped surface, especially at the apex, will lead to higher jump height. An easy way of applying this is when a player is chasing you and you are about to go over, say a staircase, simply jump and you will go over them. More situationally, you can fall between a small gap, and if it is small enough, you can jump as you land again to gain more height. A notable example is on Construction with the striped platforms and the main floor (all examples given are discussed in more detail in section two.)

1.7 - Overview of the Maps

This will be a brief overview of the four maps in the game. In section two, I will go into them more exhaustively.

Construction
Construction is the newest map as said before. It takes place on a construction site, and is by far the biggest map in the gamemode. It holds an unique feature where there are no barriers, instead you can fall down bottomless pits. When this happens, you teleport up in the sky and get ragdolled upon landing. On other maps, if you fall off the map (possible using the out of bounds tricks), you teleport in a random spawnpoint with no cooldown, same as the Battle Royale maps. There is a way of getting this normal respawn on Construction, which is covered in part two of the guide.

Construction features four wrecking balls at different spots, oil drums and crates that are scattered across the map, portals situated at opposite ends, just to name a few. All in all Construction is by far the most complex and technical map, with so many different parts to it that tricks are still being found.
Notable Features: wrecking balls, portals, stop sign, cannons, oil drums and boxes.

The Original Maps
The original three maps were released with the addition of the gamemode. I put these all under one section because they share many things in common and are part of one group, Construction is a bit of an outlier. Each of these maps have puddles you can slip on scattered across them, and have in bounds and out of bounds areas unlike Construction (Construction technically does as I have said.)

Color City takes place in a park kind of area with colourful buildings (hence the name.) The map is pretty simple, making it more difficult to juke on than any other map, as you cannot rely on tricks as heavily. All you really have are barrels you can jump on, the occasional bush, the fountain, and the stop sign obstacles.
Notable Features: Barrels, the Fountain, Stop Sign guys, and Bushes.

Supermarket takes place at a supermarket. This map, along with construction, features dynamic objects: the carts, which are objects that are movable. There are also watermelon cannons which launch watermelons that initiate ragdoll. The closeable doors on each side of the map are one of the funnest ways to mess with someone, but is annoying when the door is closed on you. The map is split in half, with both sides being mirrors of each other (not exactly, puddle arrangements slightly vary.) The map is not as complex as Construction, but has a lot of neat tricks under its belt, making it one of the most fun while also not being insanely broken as is Construction.
Notable Features: Watermelon Launcher, Bouncepad, Carts, Closeable Door.

Teleport City does not have many features similar to Color City, but it is less bare bones. Two hammers on each side provide an interesting obstacle you can use in many ways. There are portals on the four corners of the map. There are also garbage bins that work as good juking platforms. Overall despite the bareness of the map, the hammers and portal liven it up enough to still be fun.
Notable Features: Hammers, Bins, and Portals.

2.1 Settings

There are three settings which are relevant to Tag.

Player Direction is Locked to Camera (On): When on, your bean always faces the direction of your camera. When off, your bean faces the direction you press on your keys. The optimal setting for Tag is to have this on, because people changing directions is much less jarring of a change, which could influence you on how easily you get juked. Yes it is cosmetic, but it does mess with the mind a bit less when you have it on.

Stop air movement when a direction key/joystick is released (On): When moving in the air while toggled on, as you release the direction key, you stop moving. This is a bit different to Battle Royale, I believe most pros recommend keeping it off, but it also can depend on the map. In Tag, it is best to keep this on though, because when doing certain tricks, such as landing on the blue wheel on top of the wrecking ball, it is nice to be able to have precise movement within the air.

Sound (On): Sound should be on. The only reason this matters is to know when somebody is ragdolled, as being ragdolled makes a unique sound. Sometimes you cannot tell while purely looking. The most notable example is when pushing someone into a stop sign guy or looping someone on the puddle, with just visual information it can be hard to tell if you truly ragdolled them, which may lead you to making a mistake.

Graphic Settings: Be sure to optimize for FPS before looks. Adjust your graphic settings until your FPS matches your refresh rate.

2.2 The Maps in More Detail

Color City
1.0 Barrel Tech: There are barrels scattered in different places across the map in sets of two or three. These can be jumped on and jumped off of. A good rule of thumb is to jump at the middle of barrels for greater height.
1.1 Barrel OOB: You can jump off the barrel to go on top of the map here. This is a very small pocket of area, but the OOB is still useful as many players go here, and you can do it on the fly if you are good enough.
1.2 Ramp + Barrel: You can jump from the top of the ramp, then off the barrels, to gain great height. You can land on one of the barrels, or by jumping down the slope to both gain speed and avoid getting ragdolled.
1.3 Ramp + Barrel OOB: Using the aforementioned tech, you can go out of bounds. With the speed boost, this is way easier for Hunter, but is barely possible as Runner. You can either jump off then go to the side, or keep going forward. The only way of doing it as Runner is to go forward, and it is the easier method.

Supermarket
1.0 Fruit Stands and Markets: Littered across the map, you can jump on the lower part of these fruit stands, and on some of the similar looking stands.
1.1 Fruit Stand into Cannon: You can jump on the fruit stands on either side of the map next to the cannons to jump into them, as the top of the cannons have no collision.
1.2 Fruit Stand over Door: Near the closeable doors, you can jump off the fruit stands on either side to jump over the door.

Teleport City
1.0 Bins: You can jump off the bins. On one side of the bins is the handle, which provides an easier boost, which you can stand on as well.
1.1 Patched Bin OOB: Before the map patch, you could jump from the bins to the buildings, allowing you to go out of bounds.
2.0 Hammers: The hammers swing in a wheel motion. Most basically they can launch you far away from Hunters, resulting in a ragdoll.

Construction
1.0 Oil Drums: These dynamic objects come in three sizes, small, medium and big. You can use these drums in practically everything due to their varying heights and movability.
1.1 Oil Drum to Containers: You can use the oil drum to get to the normally inaccessible containers across the map.
2.0 Crates: Dynamic objects which are shorter, but easier to jump off.
