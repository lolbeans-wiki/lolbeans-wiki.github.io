1.1 - Introduction, History, and Overview

Lolbeans.io is a game released on October 14th, 2020 with the main Battle Royale gamemode. Tag came out four years later, releasing on April 10th 2025. Serial, the game’s developer, opened a play test the month before on March 7th, previewing the new gamemode. Historically, Tag started before with the Lolbeans community creating tag games using the Community Levels gamemode. People would make custom stages for tagging, and battle it out with friends. Later, it would become an official gamemode.

We all know the classic playground game tag; it is quite a basic premise, so how could this be fun when translated into the medium of video games? It is about the implementation, which is unique across all the other tag games. Its defining feature is a proximal-point system, in which you gain points the closer you are to the taggers (known as ‘Hunter’ in this game,) fighting for the most points at the end of each round.

Tag was popular at first given the excitement around a new gamemode, as well as it being school season at that time (as a lot of people play at school,) but it has seen a decline ever since due to a lack of updates and an overall fall in the general Lolbeans player base. After release, it got a minor update changing the existing maps in various ways, (I will go into this more in section two.) Then it got another map: Construction, on June 6th of the same year. It had seemed that this gamemode would be regularly updated, with more exciting maps and features as time went on; over one year later, it has yet to get another update, much to the disappointment of me and the rest of the Tag community.

One thing I will admit is that with only four maps and no updates in over a year, the gamemode has risks of getting stale, especially if you do not hold the same amount of love for it that I have. However, with discoveries still being made in terms of tricks and glitches, and the satisfaction of juking people, it can still feel fresh and amazing. Tag is easy to get into for its intuitive simplicity, but has a high skill ceiling as you need to sharpen your juking skills over time, and learn all of the tricks. With this, it has offerings for both veterans and newcomers.

1.2 - Basic Information

Now that you know the history and current situation of the gamemode, I will talk about the gamemode itself. There are upwards of eight players, with two of those eight being Hunters. Rounds begin with a three second countdown, then last for three minutes. After they end, there is a fifteen second intermission time in which it displays the final player rankings for the round. Tag goes on for as long as you stay in the server, unlike Battle Royale which plays three rounds before the game ends. This is because winning in Tag does not net coin or exp rewards like Battle Royale.

Here are the UI elements. The chat box in the top left is self-explanatory. The meter at the bottom displays the amount of points being gained (note that this disappears while you are Hunter, because you gain no points while being in this state.) On the top right is a leaderboard displaying every player and their point totals, along with their player status which is shown by the colour of the player’s name and rank—white for Runner, and red for Hunter.

The objective is to get the most points. For the first 5 seconds of the round, you gain no points, then you gain points every second, getting more points the closer you are to the Hunters at an upward of ten points per second (where you basically have to touch them.) Points can be gained in decimals as well, which you can see by hovering your mouse over the point total during the intermission.

The point mechanic is a big part of what makes this gamemode so fun. It forces players to learn juking, rather than simply hiding or trying to survive the longest. It requires both good mechanics and predictions to win.

As alluded to, there are two types of players: Runners and Hunters. Runners are the default player, and Hunters are the taggers. You can tell somebody is a Hunter by the red name and rank on the leaderboard, or by the red bubble and name tag by their character. While being Hunter, you stop gaining points. Hunters have 20% more than the default speed. Touching a Runner as a Hunter passes the Hunter status to them, stunning the person for around 2.4 seconds in what is known as a ragdoll.

There are four maps as of now: Construction, Color City, Supermarket, and Teleport City (The last three are the file names. For Construction, the file name is Construction Site, but I say Construction as that is the official name given in the update log on the website.) Each map has different obstacles and tricks which makes map knowledge incredibly important. Learn the intricacies of each map (which can just be achieved from attentive and constant play), and use them to your advantage against less knowledgeable players to come out on top. The map shifts in sets of two, meaning you play two rounds on each in the order I listed them.

1.3 - Basic Mechanics

The mechanics are deceptively simple in this game. The only movement options you have are jumping and moving. This is limiting in a way which inspires resourcefulness. It makes it so it would be even more advantageous to learn the maps in and out. The fun is utilizing tricks and glitches which stretch how far one can go with such little mechanics. You can even challenge yourself by prohibiting jumping. The jump height is deliberately less than the player height, making it so you cannot simply jump over Hunters.

There is also the ragdoll state: it is a period of inactionability (You cannot move or tag people) that lasts for around 2.4 seconds. It is called ragdoll because your physics get weird, you get down on the ground, and it leads to certain glitches. Ragdoll is primarily activated when getting tagged. You also get it from hitting special obstacles such as puddles or stop signs, getting hit by something like a wrecking ball or hammer, falling from a great enough height, or in niche cases colliding with another player or wall. It is important to note that the ragdoll state can be activated during a ragdoll, which allows you to stun players infinitely in certain cases.

Uniquely in tag, you can zoom in and out using the scroll function on a keyboard. This allows you to gain more visual information on where players and Hunters are.

1.4 - How to Win

The best way to win is to generally be gaining at least six points per second if you are a beginner. Even seven points allows you to keep a nice amount of distance. I find that there is a lot of lee way come out on top because the average player does not optimize for points. You do not need to be very mechanical or skilled to win, you just need to be calculating and smart about how you gain your points.

When tagged, go after someone who is of a lower skill level, or is pretty close to you. Try to spend as little time tagging someone as possible. One mistake that can cause people to trip up including myself is to try to tag the person in first place. This person is usually decent, and may take a while to tag, leading you to wasting a lot of time and widening the gap between your point totals even more. So overall just go for the easy person and try to catch up.

If the competition is really intense, you will need to be gaining 8-9 points to catch up. Find AFK players, farm bots if possible, use tricks mentioned in part two, or just be good enough to avoid players even from that close of a distance. As a goal to set for yourself, try to reach at least 1000 points per game on average.

1.5 - Juking

Juking skill is determined by how long you can avoid being tagged while also still gaining a good amount of points. It adds a risk reward system to the game. You can either keep your distance, losing out on a lot of points but barely getting tagged, or stay close, gaining many points but being at much more risk. This tight rope players have to walk is another component of the game that keeps it interesting. Here I will explain beginner methods that will help you get tagged less while also gaining adequate points.

The easiest principle to follow is that just like in many other games, high ground is a treasure in Tag. With short jump heights and stubby proportions of the characters, it is easy to get height over Hunters, allowing you to be close but also safe. For example, on Teleport City there is this ramp section where Hunters will have to go all the way around to get you. This is also the basis of many of the easiest tricks. The main ones being the barrel and bin jukes, where you gain height by jumping off a barrel or bin, allowing you to go over Hunters.

A more bare bones way of juking is simply tricking the Hunter into thinking you are going one way, then going the other, or just doing something unexpected like spinning around. This adds an element of meso to the game; adapt to habits and predict accordingly. This skill is important especially if the person you are being chased by has similar to or higher trick-knowledge than you. Ultimately there is not an exact science to this, juking depends of many factors such as ping, reaction time, and luck, as nobody is a fortune teller; it requires a lot of practice to get better at.

1.6 - Deeper Dive into Mechanics

There are more advanced mechanics in this game that makes the game a lot more engaging once you get a grasp on them. These are less obvious, but more powerful things to know that will make the game easier.

First is speed. Normally you cannot increase your speed, always moving at the default, but there is a way of building speed and maintaining it. On sloped surfaces, with enough height, if you jump while going down them, you gain some extra speed (at times this also stops ragdoll when falling from a great height.) You can also gain speed from things that launch you such as the hammers on Teleport City, wrecking ball on Construction, and the bouncepads on Supermarket. Now usually you lose this speed after landing, but if you keep jumping, you maintain a portion of that speed, allowing you to zoom through maps, this is called the B-Hop.

Another aspect is the jump physics, they are strange, and can be abused in insane ways and allows for otherwise unfathomable glitches. Normally a jump is pretty short, not going over players, but there are ways to make it higher. Consecutive jumps in some window of time will lead to greater height. For example, jumping from the ground and then off another object quickly enough will send you pretty high. Chaining jumps together also gives a bit more height, this most crucially allows you to more easily jump off the railings on Construction. Jumping from a sloped surface, especially at the apex, will lead to higher jump height. An easy way of applying this is when a player is chasing you and you are about to go over, say a staircase, simply jump and you will go over them. More situationally, you can fall between a small gap, and if it is small enough, you can jump as you land again to gain more height. A notable example is on Construction with the striped platforms and the main floor (all examples given are discussed in more detail in section two.)

1.7 - Overview of the Maps

This will be a brief overview of the four maps in the game. In section two, I will go into them more exhaustively.

Construction
Construction is the newest map as said before. It takes place on a construction site, and is by far the biggest map in the gamemode. It holds an unique feature where there are no barriers, instead you can fall down bottomless pits. When this happens, you teleport up in the sky and get ragdolled upon landing. On other maps, if you fall off the map (possible using the out of bounds tricks), you teleport in a random spawnpoint with no cooldown, same as the Battle Royale maps. There is a way of getting this normal respawn on Construction, which is covered in part two of the guide.

Construction features four wrecking balls at different spots, oil drums and crates that are scattered across the map, portals situated at opposite ends, just to name a few. All in all Construction is by far the most complex and technical map, with so many different parts to it that tricks are still being found.
Notable Features: wrecking balls, portals, stop sign, cannons, oil drums and boxes.

The Original Maps
The original three maps were released with the addition of the gamemode. I put these all under one section because they share many things in common and are part of one group, Construction is a bit of an outlier. Each of these maps have puddles you can slip on scattered across them, and have in bounds and out of bounds areas unlike Construction (Construction technically does as I have said.)

Color City takes place in a park kind of area with colourful buildings (hence the name.) The map is pretty simple, making it more difficult to juke on than any other map, as you cannot rely on tricks as heavily. All you really have are barrels you can jump on, the occasional bush, the fountain, and the stop sign obstacles.
Notable Features: Barrels, the Fountain, Stop Sign guys, and Bushes.

Supermarket takes place at a supermarket. This map, along with construction, features dynamic objects: the carts, which are objects that are movable. There are also watermelon cannons which launch watermelons that initiate ragdoll. The closeable doors on each side of the map are one of the funnest ways to mess with someone, but is annoying when the door is closed on you. The map is split in half, with both sides being mirrors of each other (not exactly, puddle arrangements slightly vary.) The map is not as complex as Construction, but has a lot of neat tricks under its belt, making it one of the most fun while also not being insanely broken as is Construction.
Notable Features: Watermelon Launcher, Bouncepad, Carts, Closeable Door.

Teleport City does not have many features similar to Color City, but it is less bare bones. Two hammers on each side provide an interesting obstacle you can use in many ways. There are portals on the four corners of the map. There are also garbage bins that work as good juking platforms. Overall despite the bareness of the map, the hammers and portal liven it up enough to still be fun.
Notable Features: Hammers, Bins, and Portals.

2.0 - Tips, Techs, Tricks, Glitches, and Facts

This is the second half of the guide which will showcase all of the glitches, tips, tricks, techs, whatever you want to call them in one place, as well as some fun facts. The document first looks at all of the maps more exhaustively, listing all of the elements and intricacies in more depth. Then it goes over general knowledge which can be applied on multiple or all maps. We will end with listing facts and other scattered information about the game, including bits on history, obscure mechanics, community, and some trivia. The information may be overwhelming, but I will try my best to be thorough and comprehensive to make it so that anyone can follow.

2.1 Settings

There are three settings which are relevant to Tag.

Player Direction is Locked to Camera (On): When on, your bean always faces the direction of your camera. When off, your bean faces the direction you press on your keys. The optimal setting for Tag is to have this on, because people changing directions is much less jarring of a change, which could influence you on how easily you get juked. Yes it is cosmetic, but it does mess with the mind a bit less when you have it on.

Stop air movement when a direction key/joystick is released (On): When moving in the air while toggled on, as you release the direction key, you stop moving. This is a bit different to Battle Royale, I believe most pros recommend keeping it off, but it also can depend on the map. In Tag, it is best to keep this on though, because when doing certain tricks, such as landing on the blue wheel on top of the wrecking ball, it is nice to be able to have precise movement within the air.

Sound (On): Sound should be on. The only reason this matters is to know when somebody is ragdolled, as being ragdolled makes a unique sound. Sometimes you cannot tell while purely looking. The most notable example is when pushing someone into a stop sign guy or looping someone on the puddle, with just visual information it can be hard to tell if you truly ragdolled them, which may lead you to making a mistake.

Graphic Settings: Be sure to optimize for FPS before looks. Adjust your graphic settings until your FPS matches your refresh rate.

2.2 The Maps in More Detail

Color City
1.0 Barrel Tech: There are barrels scattered in different places across the map in sets of two or three. These can be jumped on and jumped off of. A good rule of thumb is to jump at the middle of barrels for greater height.
1.1 Barrel OOB: You can jump off the barrel to go on top of the map here. This is a very small pocket of area, but the OOB is still useful as many players go here, and you can do it on the fly if you are good enough.
1.2 Ramp + Barrel: You can jump from the top of the ramp, then off the barrels, to gain great height. You can land on one of the barrels, or by jumping down the slope to both gain speed and avoid getting ragdolled.
1.3 Ramp + Barrel OOB: Using the aforementioned tech, you can go out of bounds. With the speed boost, this is way easier for Hunter, but is barely possible as Runner. You can either jump off then go to the side, or keep going forward. The only way of doing it as Runner is to go forward, and it is the easier method.
Alternatively you can use boosts similarly to the Devil’s Trick boost to get up with the help of other players. With enough boosters, you can even use the barrel on the left. On these barrels you can stay hovering between them as if you are falling, which looks pretty funny. You can also use this to tag someone as you perform the out of bounds. It seems that you carry your aerial momentum even if you lose your Hunter status, making for an easier OOB while being untagged.
2.0 OOB explored: Once out of bounds you can go along the top of the map. There is a higher area and a lower area, the higher area is where you usually do the trick, once you drop down you cannot get back up there. There are certain places you can fall into and softlock yourself. You can also go on the buildings outside the map, and jump off the map to be spawned back in bounds. There are a bunch of random barriers exclusive to this map that make you unable to access certain parts even though they are already part of the out of bounds area, colliding with some of these barriers may cause you to get ragdolled. It makes it difficult to explore and softlocks way more common as opposed to other maps.
3.0 Bush Tech: On the map there are bushes which you can jump on. These simply give you some height. They are harder to stand on than barrels (You can either stand on them with the right position or keeping running up them.)
3.1 Bush OOB: You can do the same as the Barrel OOB by jumping on this bush, it seems to be sticking out from the barrier a little bit, enough for you to jump off it. You can stay on this bush by running up it. Overall this is also just a good way of getting height even without doing the trick.
3.2 Bush Full Map OOB: If you are tagged during the jump off the bush, you can be boosted enough to go to the very top of the OOB area. This is very hard, I did it on accident once and have tried to reproduce it to get a video, but have not succeeded as of now.
4.0 Hedge Tech: Next to the stop sign guy, there is this singular hedge that pokes out through this area, you can jump off this for some easy jukes. You can stay on it by running up it.
5.0 Stop Signs Guys: These stop sign guys move horizontally back and forth, serving as roadblocks which ragdoll you if you make contact with them. You can bait people into running into them, and push the ragdolled person towards it, using the principle of stacking ragdolls.
5.1 Jump off top of Stop Sign: You can also jump off the top of the stop sign for a second, although usually it ragdolls you. This can be done on the stop signs next to the bush and hedge respectively. This is not useful at all, but something you can do.
5.2 Pushed off map by Stop Sign: You can also get pushed off the map by the stop signs, it requires specific positioning, and so far I have seen it work on two stop signs.
6.0 Fountain: The Fountain is in some ways the center of all the action of the map, as most activity happens here. The Fountain most simply acts as a Ring-Around-the-Rosey in juking. Using the principle of slopes, you can also jump while running up them to gain extra height (albeit pretty small, you can use this height to get onto a barrel.) You can also jump off the edge of the fountain, the timing is pretty tricky, but the jump is insanely high if you do it right.
6.1 Patched Fountain OOB: You used to be able to jump off the top of the fountain, leading you to the full map OOB. This was patched in the tweaks to the maps I mentioned earlier.
7.0 Ramps: These ramps follow all of the properties of the sloped surfaces. Not much else to say.

Supermarket
1.0 Fruit Stands and Markets: Littered across the map, you can jump on the lower part of these fruit stands, and on some of the similar looking stands.
1.1 Fruit Stand into Cannon: You can jump on the fruit stands on either side of the map next to the cannons to jump into them, as the top of the cannons have no collision.
1.2 Fruit Stand over Door: Near the closeable doors, you can jump off the fruit stands on either side to jump over the door.
1.3 Fruit Stands on Platforms: On the platforms are fruit stands you can jump off of, is a bit harder to do though.
1.4 Easy Fruit Stand: You can jump off this fruit stand just by jumping and hugging the barrier. It is near the area with the door.
2.0 Doors: On each side of the map is a closeable blue door with a button. The closing of the door can ragdoll players on hit, and if not, it blocks them from entering, giving you ample time to get away from them. Note that you get tagged through the door.
2.1 Camping: Once you close the door on someone, you can run to the bouncepad, then B-Hop off that, and enter the door before they get you. Good against targetters, which are players who specifically go after you ignoring other players.
2.2 Button Jump: As the button goes up you can jump off it while standing on it to jump over or on the door. This can lead to you basically locking someone in while you get away, a nice switcheroo. When on the door you should go on one of the knobs to avoid falling once the door re-opens.
2.3 Button Ball Knowledge: You can jump off the button, going over the door sometimes.
2.4 Button Ball Knowledge OOB: You can jump off the button to an invisible part of the wall you can also jump off of to go out of bounds.
2.5 Fall off Map with Door: When hit by the door but running into the corner, you can fall off the map.
2.6 Door OOB: You can get launched from the door to the top of the map, only one person has ever done this, and it has only been done once. Trust me I have been trying.
3.0 Watermelon Launcher: The button on top launches the watermelons, which ragdoll on contact. These can be used to obstruct Hunters and such.
3.1 Inside Watermelon Launcher: The watermelon launcher itself has interesting properties. When you run into it, you are pushed backwards a bit. The top of it has no collision allowing you to go inside it. You can jump from a cart or fruit stand to get into it, or jump down into it from the top of the map done through the numerous OOB tricks already mentioned. You can also be pushed into it by a player directly, or a player pushing a cart. You can leave once inside, but it takes a bit to escape from being pushed. When inside, even when someone launches a watermelon, you are not pushed out unless you are very close to exiting the Launcher already.
3.2 Watermelon OOB: Since the Watermelons go to the place of the bouncepad, you can combine them to go OOB in two ways. You can be launched by them by getting ragdolled, or jump from the bouncepad and then off the watermelon without getting ragdolled. The latter is more consistent and fun.
4.0 Bouncepad: Situated on the ends of the map, they launch you forward. You can B-Hop to maintain the speed boost. You can mess around with players by baiting them into taking the jump, you can also predict where someone will land when they use it. You can stop your jump by colliding with a barrier during the launch, leading to more mind games.
4.1 Bouncepad OOB: I already mentioned the watermelon method, but you can also use a cart to jump off as you do it.
5.0 Stairs: There are stairs on the center platform, the side platforms, and below the side platforms. These follow slope properties of course. The lower and higher stairs can get you to the side platforms in ways faster normal jumping.
6.0 Invisible wall jumps: There are seemingly random parts of the wall you can jump on, the location for all of these are yet to be figured out, I currently know of four per one side of the map: (1) next to the bouncepad, (2) the aforementioned spot near the button, (3) next to the side platform stairs, (4) near the watermelon launcher.
6.1 Invisible Wall Jump OOB: As mentioned with the button and side platforms, with a jump off these (or a cart), and then a jump off the invisible wall, you can go out of bounds.
7.0 Carts: These are movable objects which are interesting as their orientation can be changed. They can be knocked either on their side, upside down, upright, or with the handle poking up. Let me discuss these variations. Side carts give the highest jumps. The ones with the handle poking up is easier to jump on due to a lower top service. Other carts can also be jumped on but may require the chain jump.
7.1 Stair + Cart OOB: Jumping off the stairs and then off the cart makes you go very high, all the way to OOB, note that it is not high enough on the lower stairs.
8.0 Side Platforms: These exist on the sides of the map with stairs. You can jump off these platforms, and also use the stair tricks here.
8.1 Platform side thingy: On the side with the stairs, there is an area you can stand on where the stairs lower. On the front of the platform you can also jump off them combined with the lower stairs, but not stand on them.
8.2 Platform side thingy OOB: This even leads to OOB, requiring being Hunter, and a jump off an invisible part of the wall to do so.
9.0 Central Platforms: Has the buttons for the watermelon launchers, and blue things at the top.
9.1 Old Bouncepads: Instead of the stairs at this central platform, there used to exist bouncepads. A video of it showcases this. It reset ragdoll.
9.2 OOB with blue thing: You can jump from the little slope to the blue thingies to go out of bounds. This only works on the side with the taller blue thing. In the patch with the old bouncepads, these were shorter, but this trick could still be done. It makes me wonder if both sides worked back then.
10.0 OOB Explored: Once you are out of bounds, you can just go on top of everywhere in the map, as well as jump into the watermelon launchers. It is definitely the most useful out of bounds to know, as you will gain at least 7 points per second if you stay on top on everyone, and there are so many methods.
11.0 Broken Puddle: There is a puddle that I believe is exclusive to one side that simply does not ragdoll no matter what. You can use this to juke people who do not know this.

Teleport City
1.0 Bins: You can jump off the bins. On one side of the bins is the handle, which provides an easier boost, which you can stand on as well.
1.1 Patched Bin OOB: Before the map patch, you could jump from the bins to the buildings, allowing you to go out of bounds.
2.0 Hammers: The hammers swing in a wheel motion. Most basically they can launch you far away from Hunters, resulting in a ragdoll.
2.1 Hammer Velocity: If you move horizontally, or vertically opposite the direction the hammer is going, while getting hit, you avoid getting ragdolled. This allows you to B-Hop off the speed boost.
2.2 Hammer Velocity Edge: Usually you can only use the hammer velocity in one direction, where the hammer goes, but if you get hit by the edge of the hammer while doing the velocity inputs, you can get the speed boost while going sideways.
2.3 Hammer Velocity OOB: A little bit random, but after the velocity, you can go out of bounds through a lucky high jump to one of the four grass areas next to the ramp section. I also got another variation of this that involved getting a high jump and then jumping off the barriers of the fences.
2.4 Hammer OOB: On either hammer, you can go out of bounds by getting launched, this requires very good positioning. They can only be done at the right side of the north hammer and the left side of the south hammer.
2.5 High Hammer OOB: On either hammer, you can get launched extremely high, an alternative method which instead gets you to one of the four bush gardens.
3.0 High Ground Platforms: In the center of the map is a gap between these two platforms, with ramps which you go up. Despite the gentleness of these slopes, you can use the slope principles to jump over players, although you will not get much speed from jumping down them.
4.0 Portals: On the four corners on the map are portals. The colours are purple and yellow, indicating which portal they lead to. You can keep going through a portal over and over again as the tagger’s speed difference takes a while to catch up from the constant spawning out of the portals. There is a wide enough gap that you can juke people into entering the portal without tagging you. There are a lot of potential mind games here.
4.1 Portal Bush: This is the bush you spawn out of when entering a portal. The portal bushes have special properties. When you make contact, you are pushed back a bit, similar to the Construction cannons and Supermarket watermelon launchers. The most notable feature, which is shared with the cannons, is the ability to reset ragdoll upon contact. You can, for instance, stand in front of the bush, and when someone tags you, they get tagged back instantly. Hunters can get around this by tagging you from the side, or just slowly walking up to you.
4.2 Portal Bush OOB: If a player is in front of the bush, and you jump past them, for some odd reason, you just go right past the bush and go out of bounds. This also works if you are tagged with enough force to put you over the bush.
5.0 OOB Explored: This out of bounds is pretty interesting. There are no barriers like last time, but you cannot just go on the top of the map normally. In either method, you just go inside the map on the other side. However, you can go on top of the map. See the fences you cannot jump on? They actually just have higher barriers, which with the out of bounds methods, you can get to. The main method is by getting on one of the buildings (which can happen from the hammer OOB or using a portal or bush).

Construction
For construction, I will not list everything you can do with the dynamic objects (movable objects,) because it is almost limitless, I will only do so if it makes sense to have the specific dynamic object in that area.
What happens when you push dynamic objects off the map? They respawn in their designated spawn positions, much like how they do on Battle Royale and Lobby Maps.

1.0 Oil Drums: These dynamic objects come in three sizes, small, medium and big. You can use these drums in practically everything due to their varying heights and movability. Most basically you can stand on them and jump off them for height advantage. You can sort of play floor is lava by jumping between them. To be clear, the rest of tricks listed involving oil drums involve large drums unless stated otherwise.
1.1 Oil Drum to Containers: You can use the oil drum to get to the normally inaccessible containers across the map. If you are advanced, you can chain jumps together in the right way to get to an even higher part of these containers.
1.2 Oil Drum to top of Door Area: You can use either a smaller drum, rail, button, or a crate to jump from said element to a large oil drum to get on top of the area with a door. You can also use just the large drum, but you need to use the floor tile trick.
2.0 Crates: Dynamic objects which are shorter, but easier to jump off. These spawn next to the platform areas, and are the intended way to get to them, although you can also use rails.
3.0 Platform + Crate Area #1: Near the double wrecking ball area, there is a platform you can jump to using crates, rails, or drums. There are rails on this platform which you can jump off of to go to the containers. You can jump from here to the grey fences.
4.0 Platform + Crate Area #2: On the other side. Has a cannon, and buttons and dispenser for said cannon. It is important to note that Hunters can tag people standing on the edge of these platforms, including 8.0.
4.1 Near-Softlock: You can go in a hole between the button and the cannon. For a while it was thought that this was a softlock, but it was found that you could get out using chain jumps.
5.0 Normal Cannons: There are three cannons. The two cannons besides the solid one have buttons next to them. When pressed, it launches a rock which ragdolls people on contact, going pretty fast.
5.1 Normal Cannon Properties: These cannons push you. Cannons reset ragdoll, although it is pretty hard to see this (I did so by getting ragdolled on a wrecking ball then landing on the cannon). They are much like the portal bushes in these respects.
5.2 Cannon to Rail: Through whatever method you use, if you jump on top of the Cannon near the front of the map, you will get pushed, and may be able to jump on the rail, making for a nice shortcut.
5.3 Solid Cannon: On the platform with a cannon, it is a solid object that you can go inside, using either the buttons which you can jump off of, or the rails. You can use the rails both from on the platform and below it to get on.
5.4 Solid Cannon Jump: You can jump off the button and then off the solid cannon for insane height, although there is not much you can do with it.
5.5 Multiplayer Cannon Launch: If you get the rock into the chute, usually requiring two players to time it correctly, then a bunch of cannon balls spew and go everywhere.
6.0 Spawnable Containers: You can spawn containers using the same trick as the multiplayer cannon launch. This spawns it on top of the other terrain.
6.1 Spawnable Containers OOB: You can jump off the leftmost container square to get to the out of bounds door area.
7.0 Dispenser: There are two dispensers, one of them on the platform, and the other near the stop sign. You can get to these using a button or a rail, working as nice high ground.
8.0 Platform + Crate Area #3: This area has crates nearby as well, but also has containers which are normally inaccessible. Again you can get here using rails.
8.1 On containers: You can use the aforementioned drum trick, jump off the rail which is on this platform, or you can also jump off a crate and then the ledge. You can chain jumps together to get on the second highest one, then you need to be Hunter to get on the very top one using this method.
9.0 Containers Area: This area does not have crates, but shares similarities with the Crate Area #3 for having Containers.
9.1 Getting on Side Containers: You can jump off the ledge on the container you actually can stand on to get on the side containers. You can from there jump on the edge of second container to get on the very top.
9.2 Getting on Higher Containers: Using the same ledge, you can jump from there and then off the gate, to this little patch out of bounds. If you do it well enough you can get on the second highest part, and even to the very highest part if you chain jumps together. From here you can explore the containers. You can get on the back of the area if you land precisely enough.
9.3 Getting on The Very Top: If you end up not getting to the top, you can do this from where you land to the part to the right, then time a jump at the edge or jump up it like you would a slope to get on the second section. Then get to the area between the two higher platforms and the bottom platform, run off it without jumping, then instantly jump to gain higher height. This uses the fall jump, and allows you get to the very top container. This is easier as Hunter.
9.4 Another Container Method: You can jump off the side of this ramp to get to the containers, and furthermore chain jumps to get to the top part.
9.5 Going Alongside this area: You can walk from the accessible container to this area here near the gate, may trick unknowledgeable players.
9.6 Falling Through Map: There is this hole in the wall you can run off, normally you fall off it and respawn either on the area at the back of the yellow platforms, or the area next to the wrecking ball. You can actually stay inside this wall as there is a small amount of ledge, you will usually fall so you have to keep running up it.
9.7 Inside Ledge: You can go inside the ledge you use for the container tricks by being ragdolled then pushed inside. Your camera will glitch like crazy. You can have a friend tag you and intentionally push you in, or you can do it by yourself by falling from a great enough height using a wrecking ball. You can be evil and tag people and then push them in, players may not know how to get out and it will be an easy point farm. To get out, simply spam jump and go to the direction that leads to exiting the ledge.
10.0 Stop Signs: Well they are not actually stop signs but I do not know the name for them. Basically these things start as open, then close when it signals a player. Sometimes it does not come down despite a player passing it for reasons I am not sure of. Most basically you can use this to block players, if it hits them it also ragdolls them. After around 30 seconds, the stop sign opens again.
10.1 Stop Sign Launch #1: You can get launched by the stop sign as it goes up by standing on it. For the one next to the containers, you can use it go on top of the lower containers and the higher containers. The latter of which requires a slow stop sign launch which does not ragdoll you, unlike normal. This requires you to get launched while being practically on the left or right edge of it.
10.2 Stop Sign Launch #2: Using the slow stop sign launch you can get onto the top of the door area, and using the normal launch can get you to the top containers.
10.3 Stop Sign OOB: Jumping off a button and then an open stop sign can give height to go out of bounds on top of the door area.
11.0 Rails: These are literally everywhere, and using chain jump, you can jump off them. This can get you to so many places. Honestly I cannot really list them all, a lot of them are on my channel anyway, but just be creative and think of what you can do.
11.1 Rail without need of chain jump: There are certain rails which can be consistently done without chain jump. The one in front of the stripe-patterned platform, and the one behind the another striped platform near the front of the map.
12.0 Fences: Fences are the names I use for the grey gates, they are like the rails’ cousins. You can use the rail or other tricks to get onto them, or jump to them from the platform crate areas #1 and #2.
12.1 Rail + Fence Jump: You can jump from the rail then off the fence. This can get you to the door OOB area, the containers on each side, portals, and can be used for spawn point shenanigans (this is mentioned in detail later.)
13.0 High Jump With Striped Platform: This particular striped platform can net you a higher jump from the platform kind of like the stair tech. You can use this to get over either of the rails, and even jump over players.
13.1 Fall Jump With Striped Platform: You can perform the fall jump between this platform and either side of the main floor to get a higher jump. This can go over players, and for the front side it lets you go over the rail.
14.0 Portals: Portals spawn you on the other side from the sky, ragdolling you. This can be used in camping, but the developers seemed to put a measure against this by adding a wrecking ball which often makes portal users fall. You can also go behind the portal to trick even more, a game of Ring Around the Rosie.
14.1 Portal Camp Bypass: To avoid getting hit by the wrecking ball, when a Hunter goes through the portal, push them back into it instead.
14.2 On Portal: You can use either wrecking balls, rails (then jumping on the gate and jumping off the portal, or just directly from a rail if Hunter,) or directly jumping from the rail then off the gate.
15.0 Wrecking balls: There are four of these across the map. There are the ones at the front of the map, then the two wrecking balls right next to each other. These swing from back to forth, ragdolling people on hit.
15.1 Wrecking ball Velocity: Like the Hammer, you can get hit by it while moving to get a speed boost.
15.2 Wrecking ball Jump: You can jump from a rail, drum, or crate, to a wrecking ball, for an insanely high jump. This tech individually can lead to many things. If a jump is performed extremely well, and you are Hunter, you can reach the height limit this way.
15.3 On top of Wrecking ball: With the jump you can get on the blue thing that the wrecking ball hangs from. A lot of the time you will fall off if you stand still, but depending on the ball and position there are ways to stay still and not fall off. It is a nice view. Also, you can chain jumps from the wrecking ball to the top of it to reach the height limit.
15.4 To Containers: Using the front wrecking balls can get you to the containers.
15.5 On top of Portal: Using the wrecking balls next to each portal can get you on top of them.
15.6 Wrecking Ball to Cannon / Platform: You can use the wrecking ball to get all the way here.
15.7 Hit between Two Balls: You can get yourself hit between two balls, this can launch you very far. This is one of the methods which can lead to you getting the normal respawn on construction. You can also use this to get to the door Area OOB. This is also one of four ways to reach the height barrier in Tag.
15.8 Wrecking Ball OOB: While Hunter, with a very well timed jump, you can get from the wrecking ball near the Door area to the door area OOB. You will ragdoll and fall off normally, but with an assisting player you can be prevented from falling.
16.0 Open and Close Bridge: Connecting the area with the spinning thingies, this door opens and closes in a cycle of 7 seconds. When it is closed, you can actually get a boost when jumping up it high enough to go over players, although it is harder than similar tricks.
17.0 Spin Thingies: These things spin around and block people. My warning is that you can be tagged through these, but they are still useful.
18.0 Door: Using the buttons on the sides, there is a door that can be activated in the same vein as the one on Supermarket.
18.1 Camping: You can pretty much infinitely camp here, as the cool down is pretty low on the door timer.
18.2 Closing the door on Runners: Hunters can use it to their advantage to—say a player is aiming to press the button to close the door on you—press the button on your side to close it on them early, then tag them.
18.3 Button Tech: You can jump on the button with a chain jump, allowing you to jump over players and get on the other side of the door, or even go on top of the door. You can also stay on the buttons by running up them.
18.4 On top of Door: Either using the nearby rail or the aforementioned buttons, you can go on the top of the door, and keep running up it to stay there. Same as the door on Supermarket, to remain on the door after it reopens, stand on the knobs.
18.5 Door Launch: You can get launched by the door while mid-air to gain speed and b-hop.
18.6 Fall off Map with Door: Much like the tech on Supermarket, you can get hit on the corner and be forced to fall off the map. This will not respawn you the typical way on Construction but spawn you in a random position.
18.7 Door OOB: I have also yet to do this myself, but I have seen someone get launched to the Door Area OOB by getting hit by the door.
19.0 Pushing People Off: There are ways to push people off infinitely on a lot of spawn points. The game sometimes tries to prevent this by creating multiple spawn points, but you can adjust for both of them at times. Either that or there’s only one spawn point.
20.0 Spawn Point Shenanigans: There are ways to reach certain spawn points from places you are not normally supposed to. This can create huge distances between you and the Hunter out of nowhere.
  1. On the back of the front container area, you can jump off the rail to the side to respawn decently far away. Players can predict this and react in time, but usually this does fool people.
  2. From the rail and striped platform near the door area, you can jump on the back rail and then past the fence to spawn in the spin thingy area. You can do the vice versa as well by jumping from the striped platforms or nearby fence past the spin thingy area and then close enough to the aforementioned area.
  3. As mentioned, in the container area you can fall off this part, and with enough speed you go to the back area which will give you enough time to evade being tagged. Although I am not sure if this is Hunter only or not as of now.
  4. Using the wrecking ball at the very front of the map next to the portal, you can go forward towards the containers, but not land on them. If you go far enough you spawn on the back container area.
21.0 Normal Spawning: There are niche ways to get the normal method of respawning same as the other maps, rather than the standard Construction way. These are:
  1. On the end of the portals, you can go far enough to bypass spawn checks. This requires a rail + fence jump on the front portal, and being hit between two balls on the other portal.
  2. You can do the rail + fence jump method on the area with the spin thingies while being Hunter.
  3. When on the very top containers in the front of the map, you can jump off while being Hunter.
  4. You can fall off the map with the door.

Non-Map Specific Tricks
1.0 Stair Tech: Mentioned all throughout the guide, but I will mention it here just so it’s easier to refer to. Slopes have these two tricks to them: 1. You get extra height when jumping up them 2. You gain extra speed when jumping down them (although it has to be decently steep.)
2.0 B-Hop: Mentioned all throughout the guide as well. If you gain extra speed through any of the methods already mentioned, you can maintain it by jumping over and over.
3.0 Chain Jump: Chaining jumps together to lead to a higher jump is important for jumping on rails, carts, and other objects.
4.0 Fall Jump: Between two areas you can land on, if you fall while going forward, and then instantly jump back, you go higher. This is relevant in the container tricks and the tricks with the buildings.
5.0 Puddles: Puddles are on the original three maps. Most basically if you run over them, you trip and ragdoll.
5.1 Puddle Juking: You can use the puddles as a way to juke. You can play ring around the rosie with it, and bait people into slipping. This is especially effective with puddles near corners and open space.
5.2 Puddle Loop: If you bump a player while they are on the puddle, they get ragdolled again. You can theoretically loop this infinitely.

2.3 Other Information

A bunch of smaller sections focused on facets of the game that do not fit anywhere, or just are not too important. I am going over everything though, so no matter how insignificant it will be mentioned.

Botology
Bots are a part of both the Battle Royale and Tag gamemodes. Bots are some of the best ways to farm points, as their movements are predictable and exploitable. I will first talk about how bots join and operate in the gamemode, and then go over characteristics and tricks regarding bots.

As said, lobbies can be filled with up to eight players, but if there are less than five, the game will fill those slots with bots. This means there can be up to four bots in any given lobby. If you join a server with no players in that lobby, then you start on the map Construction with four bots.

You can identify whether a player is a bot with these principles:
Attire: Bots typically only wear outfits acquired through the level up system, which are very cheap. The hats they wear are very cheap as well. Anything more than like 1000 Gold is a real player.
Running Behaviour: Bots always move, they never stop. If you see a player standing still, that is real. Bots can sometimes get stuck on things that their path finding did not account for. For example they will keep running into a wall. After a few seconds they jump the other direction and keep going. They will tend to try and go near Hunters somewhat, they come close then circle back and go somewhere else for a second. Overall the behaviour is quite easy to spot.
Chasing Behaviour: Bots always chase the nearest player. Bots follow you like lasers. You cannot juke them in the same way you can a player due to their programmed behaviour without any thought to it. Bots which are chasing you also jump when you jump. This makes it so the slope tricks do not always work, however it can also lead to another thing. If you stand in front of a portal bush, and get a bot who is tagged to jump towards you, you can be knocked out of bounds without assistance of another player.
Bot Loops: This predictable behaviour can be exploited in three ways. 1. You can circle around them over and over and they will never get you. 2. In the right position, the bot will circle around you while you do nothing. 3. The bots cannot get to certain places which are higher up. Bots can only go as simple as jumping on something directly, they cannot jump from say, a box on construction to the platform above. Notably, on Construction near the front of the map, if you go on the front container area, they will completely miss you every time.
Names: Bots are never named Player, which is the default name given to players who have not set a name, or alternatively the name people see if a player’s name is censored. Also, bots cannot have emojis in their name. You can also just use your instincts to determine if a name feels “Botty”. Like if someone’s name is just “David” that screams bot to me. Using the safe usernames setting applies to everyone, so it will not help in this regard.

How are Points Truly Scored?
This is a bit restating, but I wanted to mention an important point. We now know of the first 5 seconds of no point gain, but did you know there is an extra time window of point gain when the round ends? So basically when a round ends, it displays your score before the intermission, but if you pay attention, you will realize that your point total will differ once you get to the intermission leaderboard. It seems that there is an extra second or so of point gain after the round ends. This can break ties sometimes. This means the most amount of points that can be scored is a little over 1750. I do not know the exact number due to decimals and the general ambiguity with the last point gain.

Map Changes
As mentioned, there have been changes to maps, but I would like to go into more detail in one section. These changes range from bug fixes to very significant. Throughout this guide, I have mentioned them in a way that may have gave off the impression that they all came in one update, but looking at videos at the time, this does not seem to be the case. It seems to be a series of changes, as I have seen videos with some changes made and some not. This is tricky because there is not an update log for this, so dates cannot exactly be determined, but they seem to come from April to May 2025.
Color City: The fountain was changed to not be able to be jumped on from the top, removing a method of going out of bounds. Also, in the out of bounds area, you cannot fall in a spot anymore, which is shown in this video. This probably means there were other changes made to barriers.
Supermarket: The bouncepads in the center of the map was replaced with stairs. It was news to me that the lower stairs near the platforms at the side did not exist then as well.
Teleport City: The buildings were changed so that you could no longer jump from a bin to go OOB. Also they added portals to each of the bushes. I am actually curious how portal spawning worked before this change, as I was not there when this was changed, and there does not seem to be any footage of this. This change happened at least before April 15th, judging by a video from M2.

How are Hunters Decided?
There are typically two Hunters as stated. However it is possible for there to be only one Hunter. If you start a new lobby, which is if there are no other players, Hunter is only given to one of the five. When another player joins, there then exists two Hunters. So it seems that you need at least two players to have two Hunters. However, if one of the players leaves, the Hunter status is not removed, but passed to another player (well, a bot in this case). I cannot say for sure, but I believe the only way there can be only one Hunter after this change is if a party of people join, making it so there are temporarily no other players (while they are watching an ad). This is basically the only way of losing the Hunter status without tagging someone. This also raises the question on how can there be less than five players, because I have seen it happen before. It seems to be mainly be because of parties, but it could be another factor I am unaware of.
If an existing Hunter leaves, the game gives Hunter to a random person. You are not ragdolled when this happens, which can lead to instant tags in some instances.

Kick Times
Kick times in Tag compared to any other gamemode is noticeably short, just 30 seconds.

The Height Barrier
There is a hidden height barrier in Tag that can only be reached on Construction. This can happen in four ways:
- You are hit between two wrecking balls
- You jump off a wrecking ball (hard)
- You jump from a wrecking ball then chain a jump off the top of it
- You get launched by a stop sign

Leaderboards, HTML Name Glitch
Long enough usernames do not fully show if a person has a low number of points. As one gains points, more of the username will reveal.
You used to be able to type HTML tags into your name, and the effects of the tags would be displayed on the Tag leaderboards. For example, putting <a>Marsh</a> would make your name yellow. If you did not put a closing tag, it would make every person on the leaderboard below you also have these effects. This lead to crazy results.
You could combine these as well, though remember that the character limit is 12, so you either had to have short names or few tags. It was a fun balancing act. This was patched, and there is no video footage of it, but I was there during this era. I miss it honestly. In the Player List linked last section, you may notice a player named “<h1>cardi b” who never changed their name after it was patched, they became a relic of that era.

Known effects:
- <a> - Makes name yellow
- <b> - Bolds text
- <i> - Itallicizes text
- <h1> - <h6> - Largens text to varying degrees
- <br> - Adds a gap, you could spam these up to three times in a username to make a large gap in your leaderboard name
- <img src=> - Since there’s no associated link, it would display a weird error box on the leaderboard
- <iframe> - Does something similiar
- <s> or <del> - Strikethroughs text
- <u> - Underlines text
- <meter> - Displays a progress bar
- <progress> - Displays a progress bar as well. I know one of them was animated in-game but I cannot remember which
- <li> - Would add bullet points to people’s names if I remember correctly
You could also apparently embed <script> tags, and by extension <style> tags, but with such limited character space, I doubt you could do much.
